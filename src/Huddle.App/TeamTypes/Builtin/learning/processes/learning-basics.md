# Learning basics

## When it applies

- A learner working towards one goal: a subject, a skill, a language or an exam, at their own pace.
- Practice and feedback only. Graded coursework and final grades stay with the learner and their teacher.

## Stages

1. **Ready**: the Task names the topic and states its acceptance in one line. Done when the Tutor Lead assigns it.
2. **In progress**: the Curriculum Planner writes the plan or session, or the Quiz Writer writes a quiz and its key. Done when the draft is in the Task.
3. **Verify**: the Tutor Lead checks the draft against the plan and the key, and the Grader marks the learner's answers. Done when the Tutor Lead posts an Evidence line.
4. **Done**: the Human marks the Task Done after reading the Evidence line.

## Who does what

- `tutor-lead` agrees the goal, writes acceptance lines, checks each draft and posts the Evidence line.
- `curriculum-planner` turns the goal into an ordered plan of topics and sessions.
- `quiz-writer` writes practice questions and answer keys.
- `grader` marks practice answers and writes feedback. It never gives a final grade.

## If a role is not hired

- No `curriculum-planner` or `quiz-writer`: the Tutor Lead writes the plan or quiz itself.
- No `grader`: the Tutor Lead marks the answers and says the feedback is practice only.

## Gates

- This process declares no hard gate. The Tutor Lead's Evidence line is the check, and the Human closes each Task.

## Cadence

- Follow the order of the plan, one topic at a time.
- The Tutor Lead posts a weekly note on what was covered and what needs review.
- A retro runs every 14 days.

## Escalation

- A request to do graded work for the learner is refused and goes to the Human.
- A disagreement over an answer key goes to the Human with both views written on the Task.

## Onboarding

- Note the learner's goal, level, weekly time and any course syllabus.
- Write Team Memory notes on how the learner likes to practise and where they struggled before.
- Propose a starter Task to the Tutor Lead.
